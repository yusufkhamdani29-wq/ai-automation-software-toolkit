# Workflow 07 — Invoice Chasing — Get Paid Without the Awkward Email

## The problem

An invoice is 14 days overdue and you keep putting off the follow-up because it feels confrontational. Every week of delay is cash you have already earned.

## What you need

The invoice number, amount, due date, and client name, plus an AI chat tool.

## Copy-paste prompts

### Prompt 1: Friendly reminder (7 days overdue)

```
Write a payment reminder email. Invoice [NUMBER], amount [AMOUNT], due [DATE], client [NAME]. Tone: friendly and professional, assume the payment was simply forgotten. Under 80 words. Include the invoice number and amount, and ask the client to confirm when payment will arrive.
```

### Prompt 2: Firm follow-up (30 days overdue)

```
Write a firmer follow-up email for the same invoice, now 30 days overdue. Tone: polite but direct. State the amount and original due date, ask for payment within 7 days, and mention that I will pause further work until the invoice is settled. Under 100 words. Do not threaten legal action.
```

## Steps

1. Check the invoice tracker spreadsheet first — confirm the amount and date before writing anything.
2. Send the friendly reminder at 7 days overdue. Diary the firm follow-up for day 30.
3. When payment arrives, mark the invoice paid the same day so you never chase a paid invoice.

## Tools

Any AI chat tool. The invoice tracker spreadsheet in this toolkit (or any list of unpaid invoices with due dates).

## Time saved

The time saved is small — the money is the point. A 5-minute email at day 7 typically gets paid weeks earlier than a 30-minute agonised email at day 45.
