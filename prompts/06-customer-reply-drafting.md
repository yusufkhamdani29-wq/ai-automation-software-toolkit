# Workflow 06 — Customer Reply Drafting — Answer Faster Without Sounding Robotic

## The problem

Customers ask the same five questions, and every reply takes 10 minutes because you start from a blank box — or you send a canned reply that sounds cold.

## What you need

Your 5–10 most common customer questions, your answers in rough form, and an AI chat tool.

## Copy-paste prompts

### Prompt 1: Build the reply bank

```
Here are my most common customer questions and my rough answers:
[PASTE Q&A PAIRS]

Rewrite each answer as a friendly reply under 80 words. Keep the facts exactly as I wrote them — do not change prices, times, or policies. Add a placeholder [NAME] for the customer's name and [LINK] where a link belongs.
```

### Prompt 2: Handle a new question

```
A customer asked: [PASTE QUESTION]. My policy is: [PASTE POLICY IN ONE OR TWO SENTENCES].

Draft a reply under 80 words that answers the question, states the policy plainly, and offers one useful next step. If the question asks for something my policy does not cover, say so honestly instead of inventing an answer.
```

## Steps

1. Build the reply bank once with prompt 1 and save it where you can copy from it.
2. For new questions, use prompt 2 — then read the draft before sending. Never auto-send AI drafts to customers.
3. Review the bank monthly: delete replies that no longer match your policies.

## Tools

Any AI chat tool. A text file, note, or your help-desk's saved-reply feature for the bank.

## Time saved

A careful reply takes 8–12 minutes from a blank box. Starting from a reviewed draft usually takes 2–3 minutes. The first build of the bank takes about 30 minutes and pays for itself within a week.
